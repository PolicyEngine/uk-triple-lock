# Represented ages and pension cohort audit

This audit reads the source at PolicyEngine UK [2.90.2](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/tree/3d12d26a12ccf1501ae3b3ae3522eaa80d4aa15a/policyengine_uk) and [2.118.0](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/tree/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk), rather than inferring a mechanism from projected spending. All source paths below are relative to `policyengine_uk/`. Checks describe these releases, not complete implementation of every statutory entitlement.

## Static representation and fiscal reference

The represented age is drawn once for every record whose survey age is exactly 80. Within sex, records are ordered by SHA256 of the dataset fingerprint and person id, then assigned ages 80 through 105 using cumulative-weight midpoints and ONS single-age shares in the calibration year. Age 105 represents the final 105+ projection cell. Records below 80 are untouched. Weights and records are neither split nor created. The weighted 80+ total by sex is preserved exactly; each represented age's count differs from its share target by no more than the largest record weight in that sex. This is input representation, not scaling a model result.

Represented single ages and within-year birthday months remain fixed across forecast years. Household weights change each year. A record describes a contemporaneous person of that represented age, rather than the same survivor becoming one year older each year. Its synthetic birth date is therefore inferred again for fiscal year `y` from the fixed represented age at **6 October of year `y`** and the fixed birthday-month draw. This lets successive contemporaneous cohorts replace basic pension recipients with new pension recipients without claiming to model deaths, migration, individual ageing or contribution histories.

PolicyEngine UK 2.118.0 uses the same fiscal midpoint. Its [`months_since_last_birthday`](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/blob/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk/variables/household/demographic/months_since_last_birthday.py) hashes person ids with splitmix64 salt 2, orders them within whole-age/sex strata and puts them at weighted midpoints. We reproduce this draw using calibration weights, store the float32 birthday-month input once, and pin it before forecast reweighting. The birthday hash follows upstream exactly; the dataset fingerprint scopes its private cache. [`utils/dates.py`](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/blob/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk/utils/dates.py) places month boundaries on the sixth and rounds inferred birth instants to the legal calendar day beginning at or after them. Fractional ages override birthday months, as upstream does.

The older release does not expose this birthday input or the cohort-specific pension-age timetable. Its age-based type formula is not an exact legal-cutoff oracle. The newer release can be a differential oracle when the same ages and birthday inputs are used. Upstream's [`state_pension_type`](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/blob/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk/variables/gov/dwp/state_pension_type.py) uses `months_since_state_pension_age`, derived from the inferred date of birth; it does not have a separately settable date-of-birth input.

The differential tests were run in a separate private virtual environment with PolicyEngine UK 2.118.0 and PolicyEngine Core 3.32.16. The birthday-month and legal-birth-date arrays matched upstream exactly. Actual PolicyEngine UK `Simulation` calculations agreed with `cohort_type` for all 188 synthetic records in all 16 years from 2024 through 2039: **100% agreement across 3,008 type calculations**, including the exact 5/6 April cases in 2026. Eligibility below pensionable age was read from the upstream model in each year. These synthetic tests validate the mechanism; they are not population forecasts. Both upstream differential tests passed. The production-runtime cohort suite on 2.90.2 passed its 10 property/example tests and skipped the two newer upstream oracle tests.

## Legal basic/new pension cutoffs

[Pensions Act 2014 s.1(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/19/section/1/made) excludes people who reached pensionable age before 6 April 2016 from the new pension. The birth cutoffs follow from [Pensions Act 1995 Schedule 4 paragraph 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1995/26/schedule/4): men in the relevant cohort reached pensionable age at 65, while women born from 6 March to 5 April 1953 reached it on 6 March 2016 and those born from 6 April 1953 reached it later. Thus eligible men born **before 6 April 1951**, and eligible women born **before 6 April 1953**, receive the basic type. Birth on either cutoff receives the new type. The [government's basic pension eligibility page](https://www.gov.uk/state-pension/who-gets-the-basic-state-pension) states the same boundaries.

The helper takes State Pension age eligibility from the model. It returns `NONE` below pensionable age, irrespective of date of birth. It does not replace the model's pensionable-age eligibility calculation with a side model.

The shared demographic [`is_benunit_head`](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/blob/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk/variables/household/demographic/is_benunit_head.py) and `is_household_head` default formulas choose the eldest member in both releases. The newer `is_claimant_or_partner` also ranks adults and parents and uses an age gap when inferring relationships. If head/relationship flags are supplied by the dataset, those inputs take precedence; otherwise top-code disaggregation can change a tied head or inferred claimant/partner. The engine must preserve available supplied household structure and run the resulting eligibility rules. A lack of monetary thresholds above 80 does not imply every derived structural variable is invariant.

This audit supports using represented ages as an explicit static model input with those qualifications. It does **not** establish that all awards are invariant to top-code disaggregation: cohort eligibility and claimant ranking are real dependencies. Scottish heating-payment and explicit age-addition coverage remain upstream limitations for the full rebuild.

## Additional pension and protected payments

The newer upstream [`additional_state_pension.py`](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk/blob/8090f1c19c65c9a956d282345bedf7ad9b3b8607/policyengine_uk/variables/gov/dwp/additional_state_pension.py) selects the flat-rate ceiling by the **period's** type; 2.90.2 instead selects it by the data-year type. Both upstream releases then uprate the residual by the flat-rate ratio. The engine needs to re-split each re-typed record against its chosen type's data-year ceiling and use September CPI for the residual. For `NEW`, that residual represents protected payments; it must be nonnegative and uprated by CPI on the same basis as additional pension. Below pensionable age, all three payable components are zero. For an eligible data-year record, basic + new + residual must reproduce the reported State Pension to the penny. These are input accounting constraints; aggregate fiscal consequences require full model runs.
