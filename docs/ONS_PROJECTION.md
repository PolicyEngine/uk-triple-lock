# Population projection input

The public input is [ONS's 2024-based national population projections for the UK](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationprojections/datasets/z1zippedpopulationprojectionsdatafilesuk), principal projection, released 28 April 2026. The download URL is the same UK zip used by [policyengine-uk-data's ONS demographic target builder](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk-data/blob/b45c373c6459762930d43a11bed5eaaec4131e14/policyengine_uk_data/targets/sources/ons_demographics.py). ONS corrected a 2062–63 column header on 1 May 2026; that correction does not change the requested 2024–2041 data.

Run `python scripts/build_population_projection.py` with `openpyxl` installed. The builder downloads the public zip, selects `uk_ppp_machine_readable.xlsx`, reads its `Population` sheet and writes `data/ons_npp_2024_uk_age_sex.csv`. An already downloaded official zip can be passed with `--source-zip`. No survey data are read. The accompanying provenance JSON records source and workbook SHA-256 hashes, the CSV SHA-256, licence, units and age definition. A live download records `source_mode=download`, `obtained_url` and `downloaded_at_utc`. Local reuse instead records `source_mode=local`, its declared ONS `source_provenance_origin` and `local_read_at_utc`; a local read is not labelled a new download. The extracted population counts are available under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/).

The CSV columns are `year,sex,age,population`. `year` is the mid-year, `sex` is `female` or `male`, and `population` is a count of people. Ages 0–104 are completed single years. ONS publishes 105–109 and 110+ as separate terminal groups; the builder adds them into `age=105`, meaning **105+**, so none of the oldest population is discarded. There are 3,816 cells: 18 mid-years (2024–2041), two sexes and 106 ages. The parser checks complete age and year coverage, positive or zero integer counts, source-cell uniqueness, and complete nonoverlapping coverage of the oldest tail. It fails if the workbook layout changes.

For a fiscal year beginning in April of year *y*, the model uses:

`population(FY y/y+1) = 0.75 × population(mid-y) + 0.25 × population(mid-(y+1))`.

This linear interpolation represents the fiscal year's October midpoint, three months after mid-year. It is a population-input convention; fiscal outcomes are calculated in full PolicyEngine runs. The available mid-years support fiscal years 2024–25 through 2040–41. The same conversion is applied to the calibration year and every forecast year before cell growth factors are calculated.

Age/sex raking cells aggregate these counts into five-year bands through 55–59, single years 60–79, then 80–84, 85–89 and 90+. Targets multiply the survey's anchor-year margins by the corresponding ONS cell growth factor. Thus the anchor-year targets equal its base margins, preserving the selected anchor weights. The API requires an explicit anchor or verified dataset `calibration_year` metadata; it does not infer an anchor from the first stored year.

The pilot explicitly uses **2025**, the calibration year in the [exact 1.56.16 source configuration](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk-data/blob/12a1e028afeef08d8b2d74ee03fd9de3a78b2dd3/policyengine_uk_data/datasets/frs_release.py#L53-L57). These are runtime-native 2025 weights: they do not restore the builder's calibrated weights because builder and runtime materialization use different population factors. The private artifact build manifest was gated. [CALIBRATION_ANCHOR.md](CALIBRATION_ANCHOR.md) records the public proof and limits. Weight equality at this runtime anchor does not establish preserved Housing Benefit or Pension Credit outcomes after changing ages and pension types. The inspected runtime uprates later native weights rather than recalibrating annually. Part C must obtain certified calibrated-period inputs and compare the benefit baseline before enabling the demographic treatment.

These are growth-anchored targets, and their absolute levels can differ from raw ONS counts. Ageing is an explicit opt-in treatment until validation and part C enable it for the published pipeline. Raked household weights and represented survey ages remain in the private `.cache`; the committed CSV contains only published ONS totals.

The source is the UK population, including Northern Ireland. Raking does not create geographic projections or household-survey coverage of pensioners abroad. Great Britain validation needs a GB filter on model outputs and published GB benchmarks; UK totals cannot be described as matched GB coverage.
