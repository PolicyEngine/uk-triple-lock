Pension indexation under forecast uncertainty: the fiscal saving from reforming the UK triple lock

We cost the adjusted Triple Lock, which the Budget of 28 October 2026 announced from April 2030, against the triple lock. A Bayesian forecast of September CPI and May–July earnings growth, fitted to the OBR's past forecast errors and earlier outturns, puts the median gross saving in 2039-40 at about £9bn (£9.2bn), with a little under half of paths above £10bn, if forecast errors over 2027–38 are as large as over 2011–26; without 2021–23 in the record the median is £6.8bn, and with errors the size of past outturn variation £8.0bn. We designed that model after two earlier models failed registered tests, so it rests on stated priors, not on a test. On the OBR's central path the saving is £0.5bn; on the OBR's long-term triple-lock assumption, which gives the adjusted rule no premium of its own, £10.9bn; and on average over simulated paths with earnings growth half a point lower or higher, estimated from 160 full PolicyEngine UK runs, £11.4bn or £11.9bn. DWP puts the saving at £15bn for Great Britain on a path it does not publish. This page embeds revision 12 of the manuscript, dated 12 November 2026.
